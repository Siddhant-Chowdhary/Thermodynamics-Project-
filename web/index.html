How it is calculated

Ice is pure water, so at equilibrium the liquid's water activity is fixed by temperature alone:

ln a_w = −(ΔH_fus/R)(1/T − 1/T_m) − (ΔC_p/R)(T_m/T − 1 + ln T/T_m)

Water freezes out until the remaining liquid reaches that activity. Polyols use one constant per OH group, a_OH = −2414.9 J/mol, fitted on glycerol only; NaCl uses an osmotic coefficient fitted to its freezing curve; contributions add in ln a_w.

A cell at slow-cooling equilibrium has the same a_w inside. Glycerol enters and shares the load; sorbitol and NaCl stay out, so with sorbitol alone the cell interior is as salty as in plain saline.

Equilibrium only: no cooling-rate effects, intracellular ice or toxicity. Criteria: Lovelock (1953) for 4.41 wt% NaCl; Mazur, Rall & Rigopoulos (1981) for 25 % unfrozen water.
